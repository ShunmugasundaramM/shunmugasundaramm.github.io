Indian broker apps look alike.

Before drawing anything, I looked at how the four biggest brokers present themselves on the home screen, in a notification and next to a price.

Symbol plus name

Most broker brands pair a pictorial mark with a wordmark. Two parts to learn, two parts to shrink.

Blue and green

Brand colours overlap with the green and red that already mean up and down on every price.

Friendly, then busy

Rounded marks and bright fills read well on an ad and poorly next to live numbers.

The brief

One mark that holds up at 16px in a notification and on a billboard. A palette that leaves market green and red to mean only up and down. A voice that stays calm when the numbers are red.

The idea

The dot is your money, right now.

Plain

Numbers come first and are never dressed up. Say what a price is, what it did and what an action costs.

Calm

Markets are loud; Pocket is not. Dark ground, one accent, no confetti. Red is information, never alarm.

Current

Everything live ticks in place, in monospace, so it can change without the layout moving.

A pocket is where you keep what matters and reach for it without thinking. The lime dot sits inside the O the way a coin sits in a pocket. The same dot appears in the product whenever something is live, new or yours, so the logo and the interface share one idea.

One wordmark. No separate symbol.

Three directions were explored. The one chosen keeps the name as set type and lets a single letter carry the mark: the O becomes a ring at full cap height, with the lime dot at its centre. The ring on its own becomes the app icon.

Protecting it

PCKETPartner logo

With partners such as banks and fund houses, the two marks share a cap height and are split by a 1px divider, with the height of the O as space on each side. In a sentence the name is always written Pocket.

The O, on its own.

The app icon is the ring O on a tile, in three colourways. Proportions are fixed so it reads the same on a launcher, in the App Store and as a 16px favicon.

Ink, with one lime accent.

Dark first. Screens are built from four ink steps, white type and a single accent. Green and red carry market direction only, never decoration or brand moments.

Archivo for words. Mono for money.

Two families. Archivo handles every word, expanded for the wordmark and headlines and normal width in the interface. IBM Plex Mono handles every number that can change, so digits hold their width as prices tick.

Two icon sets, two jobs.

Category icons are Pocket’s own isometric objects, used large on product tiles. Interface icons are Material Design Icons: flat, single colour, 24px, native on Android and iOS. The two never appear at the same size.
