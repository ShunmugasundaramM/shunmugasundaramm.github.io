01 · Overview

A brokerage site is four products wearing one skin.

Marketing pages have to sell. Market data has to be readable at a glance. Tools have to calculate. Legal pages have to be complete and findable. They share a header, a footer and nothing else about how they behave.

At this page count, consistency stops being a style preference and becomes the only way to finish.

Every page that needed a new component was a page that took a week. Every page assembled from existing parts took a day.

So the work was front-loaded into a kit: one grid, one type scale, four table patterns, one form pattern. The sixteen legal pages were then built from a single template in an afternoon.

05 · Market Data

The densest screens on the site.

An option chain puts calls and puts either side of a strike price, eleven columns wide, refreshing live. It is the one place where the design gets out of the way entirely: no card, no shadow, no rounded corner. Just a ruled table, tabular figures, and colour reserved for direction.

06 · Tools & Calculators

Answer first, inputs underneath.

Six calculators share one layout: the result sits at the top at display size and updates as the inputs change, so nobody has to scroll down to a “Calculate” button and back up to read what it said.

07 · Portfolio & Reports

Eleven screens for money you already hold.

Portfolio, alerts and the three statutory reports are the pages clients actually return to. They needed an empty state as carefully designed as the full one, because a new account sees the empty version first and decides from it whether the tool is worth using.

08 · Account & Support

Opening, changing and closing an account.

Eleven pages cover the full account lifecycle, including the ones most brokerages hide: modification, closure and the grievance escalation ladder. Making them easy to find is a trust argument, not a usability one.

09 · The Long Tail

Sixteen pages nobody plans for.

Privacy across twelve sub-pages, three terms documents, a disclaimer. Regulator-mandated, rarely read, and impossible to omit.

They got one template (a sticky contents rail, accordion sections, 16px body at a 72-character measure), and it took an afternoon instead of a fortnight.

10 · Outcome

The kit was the deliverable. The pages were the proof.

78 pages

Ten areas, from marketing through live market data to statutory legal text, all shipped from one component set.

4 table patterns

Option chain, holdings, reports and pricing all resolve to four ruled-table variants rather than a bespoke layout each.

1 typeface

Poppins in five weights carries marketing headlines and eleven-column data tables without a second family.

Reflection

What a 78-page site teaches you

On a small site you can design each page on its own terms and it still hangs together. Past about twenty pages that stops being true. The work becomes deciding what a page is made of, then assembling.

The mistake I would not repeat is leaving the legal section until last. Sixteen pages of statutory text arrived as an afterthought and turned out to need the most careful typography on the site, because nothing about them is scannable unless the type does the work.

Design the kit.
Then the pages are just assembly.
