02 · The Challenge

Nobody buys research from a company that looks new.

Three problems shaped every decision that followed.

01

No track record to show

Founded in 2020, with no client logos, case studies or press to lean on. Credibility had to come from craft and clarity instead of proof.

02

Five service lines, one story

Research, content, technology, broking and managed services. Listed flat, the offer reads as a vendor for hire rather than a specialist partner.

03

A cautious audience

Compliance-minded buyers scanning for competence. The site needed to feel measured and institutional, not like a startup landing page.

03 · The Mark

A bridge,
drawn on
a grid.

The name is the idea. An arch with even piers reads as a bridge at any size, and the same arc splits to hold the wordmark, the enclosing circle doubles as the span. Built on a fixed grid so the mark stays true from a favicon to a trade-show banner.

Construction, arc, nine piers, deck

Three grounds, one weight

Dark on white for documents and reports, white on teal for the brand's own surfaces, and light on black where the mark sits over imagery.

06 · Structure

Five pages,
one path.

Rather than a single page carrying all five service lines, each got its own room with a consistent shape: banner statement, plain-language explanation, then the products inside it. Every page ends in the same contact form, so intent is never more than one scroll from an enquiry.

07 · Home

Lead with
the work,
not the pitch.

The hero names the service and the asset classes in one line, sell-side research across equity, commodity and fixed income, then a single call to action. Below it, the five service lines get equal weight on a light ground, so a visitor can find their own reason to stay before any persuasion begins.

One statement, one action
Headline, two short paragraphs, Register Now. Nothing competes with it.
Services as a flat grid
Five lines, equal weight, no ranking, buyers self-select.
Who we are, then contact
Credibility before the form, and the form on every page.
08 · Service Pages

Same shape,
three times.

Each service page opens with a teal banner carrying the promise, explains the thinking in plain paragraphs, then breaks the offer into a card grid. Repetition is the point, once a visitor learns one page, they can read all three.

01 · Research
White-labelled products
Seven report and tool types, each a card a broker can point at.
02 · Content
Digital presence

03 · Technology
The broking stack

09 · About

Put faces
to it.

For a firm with no track record, the people are the track record. The About page states the mission on a full-width teal band, then shows the whole team and the directors by name.

It is the one page where the brand raises its voice, everywhere else, the teal is an accent.

10 · Where It Landed

A firm that
reads as
established.

The identity and the site shipped together, which meant the mark, the type and the colour all arrived in the same first impression.

A
One mark, every surface
Three ready variations meant the team could put the logo on a report, a banner or a video without asking for a new file.
B
Five offers, separately sellable
Giving each service line its own page turned a flat list into pages the sales team could send on their own.
C
An enquiry on every page
The same contact block closes all five pages, so no route through the site ends without a way to get in touch.
11 · Reflection

What I'd
change.

The service pages carry a lot of copy in centred columns. Left-aligning the body text and tightening the measure would make them faster to scan, and several cards repeat the same sentence about combined analyst experience, that space would be better spent on what each product actually contains.

I would also push the stock illustrations out in favour of the firm's own report layouts. Screenshots of real output would do more for credibility than any illustration can.

The mark is the part I would keep untouched. It survives at a favicon, works in one colour, and the idea is legible without explanation.
