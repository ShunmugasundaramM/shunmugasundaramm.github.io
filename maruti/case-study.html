UX case study · Connected car ownership · India

One app for the whole life of a Maruti car

A Maruti owner with a Suzuki Connect car today needs at least two apps: one for the car and one for service. This study maps that split, finds where owners get stuck, and proposes one owner experience across the phone and the head unit.

Independent concept. This is not a Suzuki or Maruti Suzuki project. Every screen is my own design with sample data, and no Suzuki screenshots, logos or artwork are used. Research is secondary: public pages, store listings and a Maruti service video, read in November 2026. I have not used the apps on a real car and have not interviewed owners yet, so findings are hypotheses with a plan to test them in section 09.

01 · Summary

What I found and what I would change

The owner has to run several apps

Maruti lists Suzuki Connect, the new Maruti Suzuki app, a legacy Rewards app and five Smartplay and parts apps. Ratings run from 2.6 to 4.8. Tata chose the opposite and merged its apps into one in iRA 2.0.

The same friction repeats in each app

Sign-in and activation fail in both: "No Records Found" for unregistered numbers in one, an OTP loop in the other. Failures end with a phone number or an email address.

The car knows things the service app does not

Odometer, health check and location live in Suzuki Connect. Service reminders, estimates and insurance alerts live in the other app. Joining them is the opportunity.

Six design moves, shown in sections 07 and 08

  1. One sign-in, one car. The car layer appears only when the car has Suzuki Connect.
  2. Home shows the three numbers an owner needs: range, kilometres to service, days to insurance.
  3. Honest commands. Four states and a reason when the car cannot be reached.
  4. Service that uses the car's data, shows fees before you commit, and tracks the workshop live.
  5. The same timeline on the head unit, with a driving mode that shows less.
  6. A free care layer and a premium car layer, with calm, clear states when a car has no device or its plan has ended.

What the real apps do well

Suzuki Connect has a strong, clear car view and rich live data. The Maruti Suzuki app covers many owner tasks in a few cards, and flags rush days when booking.

What neither does

Neither connects the car's own data to service, or shows the owner one list of what needs attention. Neither designs for the moment something fails.

The risk to avoid

The Maruti Suzuki app's home leans toward selling cars and accessories. My version keeps the owner's own car at the centre and leaves shopping a tap away.

04 · Findings

Seven problems worth solving

Evidence labels say how much I could verify. "Official" is a Maruti page. "Reviews" are a few store reviews I read, so none of these is a measured rate.

1

Remote commands fail without an explanation Reviews

A 31 Oct 2026 Play review says lock often shows "unsuccessful" and location often drops to "connectivity lost". A 2022 review with 229 helpful votes says live tracking sometimes does not work, which matters most to owners worried about theft.

Suzuki Connect Play listing
2

Sign-in and activation break in both apps Official, reviews

Maruti's subscribe page says a number not registered with Maruti cannot subscribe, shown as "No Records Found". A 23 Oct 2026 review of the Maruti Suzuki app describes about 20 OTP attempts that loop and disconnect, ending with a phone booking. Both apps need the owner's number to match the car record.

Maruti subscribe page · Maruti Suzuki Play listing
3

Renewal can dead-end Reviews

A 4 Sep 2026 review describes a lapsed plan, then a payment step that lands on a blank page and a "checking your previous transaction" loop. Plan prices (₹2,899, 5,299, 7,799) are on one page, but a reviewer still expects to pay "over 9000".

Suzuki Connect Play listing · Maruti subscribe page
4

Costs surprise owners at purchase and at the workshop Reviews

October and November 2026 reviews of the Maruti Suzuki app describe an accessory bought in the app that the dealer said did not fit the car, a long delivery gap, and fitting charges not shown when ordering. The in-app cost calculator exists but the checkout does not carry that clarity.

Maruti Suzuki Play listing
5

Support always ends at a phone number Reviews

Developer replies in both apps point to toll-free numbers or an email address, including for payment and renewal failures. Nothing in the replies I read opens a ticket in the app.

Both Play listings
6

Care and car data are split Inferred

The service app sends alerts for PUC and insurance and estimates for service. The car app holds the odometer, health check and location. I found nothing that shows one feeding the other, so service reminders cannot use real kilometres from the car.

Both Play listings
7

Data use is not explained in plain words Official, market

Both Play listings say the app may share personal info with third parties; the Maruti Suzuki app lists financial info as well. Deloitte India found 72% of consumers worry about vehicle-location data and 73% about device data. India's DPDP Rules, published Nov 2025, bring consent and erasure duties from about May 2027.

What owners say beyond the apps

Older forum posts, complaint sites and survey reports. These are signals, not measured rates, and several are years old.

Strengths

Owners trust the service network

  • JD Power ranked Maruti first for dealer service satisfaction for an 11th year running, scoring 849 against an industry average of 812. The study is old; Hyundai led in 2017 and I found no 2026 ranking.
  • A 2018 Team-BHP owner praised the escalation process: a genuine complaint gets action.
  • A Feb 2025 CarDekho review of a Baleno service was very positive and called the centre clean and efficient.
Frictions

The pain is time, cost and the digital steps

  • JD Power says owners want service done faster after drop-off, with doorstep service and alternative transport. Clear, frequent contact from the service advisor matters more each year.
  • A 2021 Team-BHP post calls Nexa's digital service process time-consuming and over-dependent on process steps.
  • Complaint-site posts describe bills that differ from the work listed, and a booked service with no slot offered for days. These are unverified single accounts.
  • A forum comparison describes several visits for the same complaint.

Owners already ask for one app

An iOS review of the older Rewards app asks to add a second car so all service history sits in one place. Another reviewer likes saving insurance for renewal, booking appointments, asking for roadside help and storing documents with expiry dates. Others call that app slow with an outdated look, and an April 2026 Play review reports no service history and failed booking.

Suzuki Connect has little public owner discussion

Beyond the Play reviews I found no recent forum threads. Maruti's phone menu routes Suzuki Connect on one option and subscription enquiries on another, and its own pages give the feature count as both "over 40" and "60+". A global Smartcar survey (Europe and US, so only a proxy) found 76% of owners had not subscribed to any paid connected service and 61% felt theirs lacked features. Owners most often asked for emergency help, maintenance and tracking, and navigation.

Reading the high ratings honestly

Suzuki Connect holds 4.8 and the Maruti Suzuki app 4.6. A high average can hide a vocal minority, and I have no figures on how often commands fail or how many owners renew. Those are the first things to measure.

05 · Problem framing

Who this is for and what they are trying to do

Problem statement

Maruti owners, more than half of them first-time buyers, must switch between apps with separate sign-ins and separate car records, and each app fails into a phone call. They cannot trust one place to tell them the truth about their car and its next due date.

Proto-persona · hypothesis

Anita, 34

Gurugram, working parent. Wants to know the car is locked in a basement and to book service without calling. Comfortable with apps, short on time.

Proto-persona · hypothesis

Ramesh, 52

Tier-2 city, first car. Worried about theft and missed insurance dates. Prefers Hindi, expects a call to fix things, and will not read long screens. His son's number is on the invoice.

Proto-persona · hypothesis

Karan, 29

Bengaluru, new-tech buyer. Compares the app with Hyundai and Kia. Wants one login and software that keeps improving. EV needs would be checked with Suzuki.

Jobs to be done

  • When I park somewhere I do not trust, help me know the car is secure.
  • When service is due, help me book, approve and pay without calling anyone.
  • When a date is coming, help me stay legal and covered.
  • When someone else drives, help me share access and take it back.

How might we

…let an owner sign in once and see every task for their car?
…use the car's own data to say when service is really due?
…make every failure explain itself and offer a next step in the app?
…show every fee before the owner commits to a booking or a purchase?

Scenario 1 · Service day

Home says "Service in 480 km". Anita books a quiet slot, drops the car, sees "Inspection" on her phone, approves one extra job, and pays at pick-up. The car's health check is attached to the booking.

Scenario 2 · Insurance week

Ramesh's son is the number on the invoice. He is added as a car user and gets the reminder too, so the date no longer depends on one phone.

Scenario 3 · Basement lock

Anita locks from the lift. The app says "Not confirmed, car last seen 6 min ago" and offers to notify her when the car connects.

Scenario 4 · Into the car

Karan picks a service slot at his desk. In the car, the head unit shows the next due item and offers to navigate to the workshop.

06 · System thinking

How the pieces connect, and why commands fail

This is my working model from general telematics knowledge, not from Suzuki documents. Engineering would need to confirm it.

Command states the app should show

  1. Sent. The phone reached the cloud.
  2. Reached your car. The unit received it.
  3. Done. The car confirmed, with a time.
  4. Could not finish. With a reason and a next step.

Design principles

  1. Show data age before data.
  2. Never claim success the car did not confirm.
  3. Show every fee before commitment.
  4. One sign-in, one car record.
  5. Consent in plain words, in the owner's language.

08 · Free and premium, and the risks

One app, two layers: what is free, what is paid, what can go wrong

This is my proposal for how a merged app could be split. The split and the plan states are for Maruti's business to decide; the risks come from the evidence in sections 02 to 04 and from my own reasoning, not from Maruti data.

Free · care layer · every Maruti car
  • Service history, booking, live workshop status, approvals
  • Insurance, PUC and registration dates, challan and FASTag status
  • Documents and who has access to the car
  • Safety, always free: roadside help, SOS and recall notices
  • Plan state and support chat
Premium · car layer · cars with Suzuki Connect
  • Remote controls
  • Live location, geofence and tow-away alerts
  • Trips and driving score
  • Alert and trip history
  • Free for the first 3 years with the device, then ₹2,899 to ₹7,799 on the current plans

Rule of thumb: only features that cost money to run every month sit behind the paywall. Nothing that is free today moves behind it. Safety stays free so the paywall never feels like a ransom.

What each owner sees, by plan state

StateCare layerCar layerWhat the app says
No deviceFullNot shown as locked. One calm card."Add Suzuki Connect to lock, find and track your car from here."
Free yearsFullFull"Free until 18 Jun 2029" in the Me tab, so the end is never a surprise.
Paid and activeFullFullRenewal date and what the plan did for the owner.
ExpiredFullRemote controls off. Last known data stays readable for 90 days (my proposal)."Your car plan ended. Renew to lock and track again. Roadside help still works."

Pros and cons of merging

Pros
  • One sign-in and one record of the car, so the sign-in problems are fixed once.
  • Car data (odometer, health) feeds service reminders and bookings.
  • A free care layer brings owners back monthly, giving the premium layer more chances to prove its value before renewal.
  • One support route, one design style, one notification system.
  • Fits the head unit idea: one story across phone and car. Tata took the same route with iRA 2.0.
Cons
  • Migration risk: both apps' users must move with their plans and history. One complaint says trip data stopped updating after the 2022 app migration; it is a single case.
  • One outage can block service booking and remote lock at the same time.
  • The app can bloat and slow down, and its home can drift toward selling.
  • Two teams, two data systems and two billing set-ups must be joined.
  • More data in one place raises the stakes under India's DPDP rules.

Where owners could get stuck, and the guard for each

RiskWhat the owner feelsGuard in the design
Locked tab with no reason"The app is broken."Say why in plain words: not on your car, or plan ended. Say what still works.
Unclear plan state"Am I paying or not?"One plan card with state and end date, always in the Me tab.
Ownership edge cases"My son's number is on the invoice." "I sold the car and still see it."Link a car with a dealer-issued code, add family users, and include a transfer-of-ownership flow that clears the old owner's location history.
One login, one failure point"I cannot sign in, so I lose everything."Fallback sign-in with a dealer code, plus in-app support chat.
Slow on cheap phones and weak networks"It never loads."Light first screen, cached care data that works with no signal, performance budget tested on low-end Android.
Notification overload"Too many alerts, so I mute them all."Rank by urgency, group care and car alerts, let owners mute by type.
Paywall backlash"They took away what I had."Keep today's free features free. Only add paid value, never move free value.
Language and age"I cannot read this."Hindi and English at launch, large text, short sentences, tested with owners over 50.
09 · Validation plan

How I would test this, and how we would know it worked

Primary research, 4 weeks

  • Interviews: 5 to 8 owners per persona, in Hindi and English, about the last time a command failed or a booking went wrong.
  • App walk-throughs: watch owners sign in and book a service in both current apps, and log every stall.
  • Field test: lock, locate and share in basements, malls and highways with a Suzuki Connect car, timing each command.
  • Dealer and service desk visits to watch activation and approval.
  • Review mining: code 500 store reviews from both apps into the seven themes and size them.

Usability test, 3 to 5 users per round

  1. Sign in with your number and find your car.
  2. Lock the car. It did not work. What next?
  3. Book a service for Tuesday and say what you will pay at most.
  4. The workshop found extra work. Decide what to do.
  5. Find your insurance date and add your son as a user.

Someone other than the designer facilitates. Record completion, time on task, errors and a short SUS score.

10 · Roadmap

What to ship first

Written as slices a Product trio could pick up in agile sprints. Effort needs engineering input. I do not know how the two apps are owned or whether a shared account exists.

Now · stop the stalls

  • Sign-in fix: let a family member link a car with a code
  • Freshness chip and four-state commands
  • Renewal page fix with a 48-hour hold
  • Show fitting charges before checkout
  • One plan card, and clear no-device and plan-ended states

Next · join care and car

  • One home with range, service and insurance numbers
  • Car data attached to service bookings
  • Workshop status with approval cards
  • Value summary before renewal

Later · one experience

  • Head unit tile and driving mode
  • In-app support chat in Hindi and English
  • Consent centre aligned to DPDP duties
  • Review of the five companion apps

What I would want to learn first

Is the main failure weak coverage, the unit sleeping, or the app's status handling? Do owners see the two apps as one product or as two? Which of the seven problems do owners rank highest? The answers decide the order above.

Limits of this study

I viewed the apps only through public store text, reviews and Maruti's videos. I did not see the apps' own screens in use, so I describe no layout of the current apps. Reddit, Quora and Facebook were not reachable, so owner voice rests on a small review sample. Some figures come from single secondary sources and are marked.
