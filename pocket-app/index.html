Project overview

Introduction

Pocket is a stock and mutual fund app for India’s newest investors. Stocks, F&O, mutual funds, IPOs, bonds and MCX sit in one app, with ₹0 brokerage on delivery and a flat ₹10 for intraday and F&O. This case study follows the work from interviews and a survey to the hi-fi screens, a working build and a usability test.

Design thinking

Design Strategy

I followed design thinking: understand people, define the problem, sketch, prototype and test, then go round again wherever testing found a gap.

Every loop started from what people said. Interviews and a survey set the problems, and the prototype test checked whether the screens solved them.

Problem Statement

People said they don’t know enough to invest with confidence. They switch apps for news and for trading, struggle to read trends or buy and sell signals, and have no one to ask in the moment. Some lost money on old information or on stocks that were later delisted, and many only learnt the charges after the trade.

Solution Offering

What Pocket does about it: a guided first fund and a plain line of explanation on every trading screen; news, prices and orders in one app; charts you can drag across or hear as a list; Pocket Research calls that show their full track record, losses included; suspended stocks marked in your holdings; and ₹0 delivery brokerage, with every charge shown before you place the order.

Understanding Requirements

Before the survey I spoke with stakeholders one to one, each answering the same set of questions, so the survey would test what the business needed to learn as well as what users needed.

What the survey showed

The survey confirmed what the interviews suggested: the biggest problem is not knowing enough about the stock market. The main issues were:

01.

Confusion between Trading & Investment

Users struggle to distinguish between trading and investment, indicating a need for clarity in these concepts.

02.

Limited Knowledge about Stock Brokers

Users lack information about stock brokers, highlighting a potential hurdle in selecting suitable platforms for their financial activities.

03.

Limited Understanding of Stock Exchanges

A significant portion of users lacks understanding regarding how stock exchanges operate, indicating a need for educational resources on this topic.

04.

Inadequate Awareness of Market Analysis

Users face challenges in analyzing market trends, emphasizing the necessity for tools or guidance in market analysis.

05.

Uncertainty in Stock Selection:

Users express hesitation in choosing stocks, underscoring a need for resources that provide insights into effective stock selection strategies.

By addressing these user problems, the trading and investment platform can enhance its user experience and empower users with the knowledge needed for informed financial decisions.

Common Trends

  • All platforms offer mobile apps for on-the-go trading/investing.
  • Educational resources to varying extents, targeting users with different levels of expertise.
  • Efforts to simplify the user experience and onboarding process.

Challenges

  • intense competition in the brokerage space.
  • Maintaining a balance between innovation and reliability.
  • Addressing customer service concerns.

Conclusion

Each platform caters to a different segment of users, offering a mix of pricing, features, and services. The choice depends on the user's preferences, trading/investment style, and comfort with technology.

  • Hidden charges only show up in the contract note

Sketching & Wireframes

I start with low-fidelity sketches and refine them into hi-fi. The research decided what each screen had to hold; the wireframes settled navigation, icons and touch targets before any colour went in. Nineteen rounds later, they became the hi-fi screens.

Open the wireframes

In the app

After the hi-fi designs I built Pocket as a working Android app. Testing the build showed what was still missing, so I added these screens in November 2026. Every screen here is rendered from the app's own code.

Money, one tap away

Groww shows the balance where you add money, and Kite keeps it in Funds. Pocket now does both. Home shows your cash beside what you have invested, with Add funds next to it. Account opens on the same cash card, with Add funds and Withdraw.

Markets you can open

The index strip, Top movers and the news on Home now open full screens, with live prices. Orders has an Executed tab for trades that filled today.

Opening an account

People can now open an account from the first screen. F&O needs proof of income, so the KYC flow asks which segments you want and fetches the proof through an Account Aggregator, or takes an uploaded document.

On the web and desktop

Pocket is coming to the web as well. I started with the part people most often finish on a laptop: opening an account. Desktop keeps the phone's six stages in a fixed rail on the left and puts each step in one narrow column, so the form reads the same on both.

Opening an account at 1280 × 800

Each of the 12 account-opening states is drawn in dark and light. Next on desktop: the watchlist, chart and order pad side by side.
